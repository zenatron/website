## A bar that works out what you mean

Closed, NotchApp is a black shape that blends into the hardware, with a few live Glances in the wings. Hover
and it springs open, and the cursor is already in the search bar. That bar is the heart of it: describe a file
the way you remember it ("the pitch deck I sent Maya last week") and it finds it. Ask a question and it reads
the top pages and answers with numbered sources. Type a sum, a conversion or a time zone and the answer is
there before you finish.

It does things too. `timer 10m`, `add lunch with Sam friday 1pm`, `move my 3pm to 4`, `dark mode on`:
one safe action just happens with an Undo, and anything bigger is shown for you to confirm. ⌘K on an app or a
file lists everything you can do with it. A colon opens the emoji picker. It can answer questions about what's
on your screen, carry an answer on as a chat, and, if you turn it on, remember what you've seen so "that error
in Terminal yesterday" finds it again.

It runs on whatever model you like: Apple Intelligence or Ollama on the Mac, your existing Claude or ChatGPT
plan through their command-line tools, or your own API key. Without one, the files, links, weather, commands
and instant answers all still work.

## Liquid Glass

On macOS 26 the open notch is Dynamic Glass: black where it meets the hardware, melting into glass below and
moving with the notch as it springs open. It comes in four levels, from Tinted to Clear. Pages slide on Core
Animation with Smooth, Snappy or Bouncy springs, and the search bar wears Apple Intelligence's glow. The demo
above is the real app over a moving backdrop, so you can watch the glass bend what's behind it.

## Everything else in the notch

**Home** is a row of cards (now playing, today and the week, weather by the hour, stats, batteries, a world
clock, your agents) that you rearrange right in the notch. **Glances** sit beside the closed notch, up to five
a side, and **alerts** come in three tiers: Flashes in the wings, Banners that drop down and queue, and Prompts
with buttons that fold away until you're ready.

**Meetings** get a Join button beforehand, a call timer and mute button beside the notch during, and, if you
ask, a recording transcribed on the Mac with notes and action items after, the other side's voices told apart.
The **Shelf** catches screenshots and downloads, keeps a clipboard history, converts video and images, and
summons a little tray if you shake a file mid-drag. There's also a per-app volume mixer, live captions, a
teleprompter, Ramble (talk it out, get tasks back), window snapping, Shortcuts and Siri actions, and a `notch`
command plus an MCP server so coding agents can report progress or ask you a quick question.

## How it's built

Native Swift, with SwiftUI and AppKit for the interface and Core Animation for the glass and the motion.
Stats read the SMC and IOReport directly; the mixer is a Core Audio process tap; on macOS 15.4 and later
MediaRemote is reached through a small dylib loaded by perl. It targets Apple silicon and builds with the
Command Line Tools alone, with no Xcode project.

The screenshots and the demo come from a demo build that runs on made-up data in its own sandbox, driven by the
app's debug hooks and recorded with ScreenCaptureKit, so none of it is a mock-up.

<Callout type="note" title="Apple silicon only">
Builds come from the Command Line Tools alone, which can't make universal
binaries, so NotchApp runs on Apple silicon Macs only.
</Callout>
